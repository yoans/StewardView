A church that takes stewardship seriously can go far.

How it works

Three ledgers, one picture

Checking
The bank

We import the bank CSV. That is the cash in the account. Starting balance plus deposits minus withdrawals.

Funds
The purpose

Givelify gifts credit funds. Plate and checks do too when we tag them. Expenses come out of a fund, usually General.

Budget
The plan

Categories on each transaction tell us how the month compares to what we planned.

Fund totals should match the checking book balance. If they do not, we look until they do.

Transactions

Every row can be categorized

Income and expense sit on the same list. We assign a budget category and a fund. A Givelify bank deposit is labeled Various. Those gifts already went to funds when we imported giving.

Receipts

Offering. Online contributions. Interest.

Disbursements

Preacher salary, payroll, insurance, utilities, maintenance, missions, LTC, supplies, and the rest of our workbook lines.

StewardView

Funds

Purpose money stays visible

Categories say what kind of activity it was. Funds say what the money is for: General, Missions, Building, Benevolence, Youth.

  • Online gifts map from the Givelify envelope to a fund. Unmatched gifts go to General.
  • If we move money out of a purpose fund, we tell leadership. Donors gave that for a reason.
  • You can open a fund and see what is in it before we approve a spend.
StewardView

The monthly plan

We set amounts. Then we live with them.

Build Budget is a list of categories with a dollar for the month. Budget vs. Actual shows what we planned and what happened. Year to date is the longer view.

Good questions

Are utilities where we expected? Can the building fund cover this repair? Is mission support current?

A good decision

Room in the plan, money in the right fund, and a clear reason that serves the church.

Your part

Treasurers enter. Elders see and ask.

  • Before a meeting. Open Dashboard, Budget, and Funds.
  • In the discussion. Put the category or fund on the screen.
  • When we approve a spend. Confirm the plan and the fund.
  • Afterward. The treasurer keeps the books. You keep the questions.

View access is enough. You are here to shepherd the work, and the numbers help you do that.
